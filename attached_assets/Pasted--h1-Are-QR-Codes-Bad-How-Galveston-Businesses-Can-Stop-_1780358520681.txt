<h3>01 WHAT IS QUISHING? // THE THREAT DEFINED</h3>
<p>Quishing is a phishing attack that uses a QR code as the bait. Traditional phishing uses links in emails. Quishing hides those links inside a QR code image. </p>
<p>Most email filters can&#39;t &quot;read&quot; an image. They miss the threat. When a staff member or customer scans the code, they are sent to a malicious site. This site might look like a login page for Microsoft 365, a bank, or a payment processor. </p>
<p><strong>THE GOAL:</strong> Steal credentials. Install malware. Redirect payments. </p>
<p>// <strong>REALITY CHECK:</strong> A QR code is just a link in disguise. If you wouldn&#39;t click a suspicious link, don&#39;t scan a suspicious code.</p>

<h3>03 HOW IT HAPPENS // ATTACK VECTORS</h3>
<p>Scammers are creative. They use multiple angles to get inside your systems.</p>
<p><strong>01 // PHYSICAL TAMPERING</strong>
The most common method in Galveston. A physical sticker is placed over your legitimate QR code. This is easy to do in a crowded restaurant or busy sidewalk.</p>
<p><strong>02 // EMAIL INFILTRATION</strong>
Your employee receives an email. It looks like it’s from HR or a vendor. It says there is a problem with their payroll or a pending invoice. It includes a QR code to &quot;verify identity.&quot; Once scanned, the hacker has their login.</p>
<p><strong>03 // MALICIOUS WI-FI</strong>
A flyer in your shop offers &quot;Free High-Speed Wi-Fi.&quot; The QR code connects the user to a rogue hotspot. This allows the attacker to intercept everything the user does online.</p>
<hr>
<h3>04 RED FLAGS // IDENTIFYING TAMPERED CODES</h3>
<p>Precision is your best defense. Train your staff to look for these signs:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>STREET STICKERS:</strong> Does the QR code look like it was printed as part of the sign? Or is it a sticker slapped on top?</li>
<li><strong>URL DISCREPANCIES:</strong> When you scan a code, your phone shows a preview link. Does it match your business name? If your site is <code>galvestoneats.com</code> but the link is <code>bit.ly/secure-login-2026</code>, stop. </li>
<li><strong>URGENCY:</strong> Does the sign or email demand immediate action? &quot;Scan now or account will be deleted.&quot; This is a classic scam tactic.</li>
<li><strong>UNSOLICITED CODES:</strong> If you didn&#39;t ask for it, don&#39;t scan it. No bank or government agency will ever ask you to verify your identity via a random QR code.</li>
</ul>
<p>// <strong>DISCRETION IS KEY:</strong> If a code looks suspicious, don&#39;t just ignore it. Remove it. Protect your customers.</p>
<hr>
<h3>05 DEFENSE PROTOCOLS // ACTIONABLE STEPS</h3>
<p>You need a plan. <a href="https://mahoneytechsolutions.com/services">Small business IT support</a> isn&#39;t just about fixing broken printers. It’s about building a wall around your data.</p>
<p><strong>01 // SECURE YOUR PHYSICAL SPACE</strong>
Check your QR codes every day. Make sure they haven&#39;t been covered with stickers. Use permanent signage (glass-etched or high-quality printing) that can&#39;t be easily tampered with.</p>
<p><strong>02 // MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION (MFA)</strong>
Enable MFA on everything. Email. Banking. POS systems. Even if a scammer steals a password via a QR code, they can&#39;t get in without the second factor. This is the single most effective way to stop a breach.</p>
<p><strong>03 // EMPLOYEE TRAINING</strong>
Your staff is your first line of defense. Run a 10-minute briefing. Show them what a fake code looks like. Tell them: &quot;Never enter a password on a site opened by a QR code.&quot;</p>
<p><strong>04 // USE SECURE TOOLS</strong>
Only use the built-in camera app on iPhones or Androids. Never use third-party &quot;QR Scanner&quot; apps. They often contain malware themselves.</p>
<p><strong>05 // NETWORK SEGREGATION</strong>
Keep your guest Wi-Fi separate from your business network. If a customer scans a bad code on your guest Wi-Fi, it shouldn&#39;t be able to reach your office computers or POS system.</p>
